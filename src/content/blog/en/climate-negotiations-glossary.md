Reading coverage of a climate summit, it is normal to meet three acronyms in one sentence: "As parties update their NDCs in light of the GST, the NCQG is also on the table." That sentence means a lot to specialists and very little to most of us. Yet the ideas behind the jargon are not that complicated. With COP31 opening in Antalya on 9 November 2026, I have gathered the most common terms in one place, in plain language, so the headlines are easier to follow. It is built as a glossary: read it end to end or jump straight to the term you need.

## The framework: convention, agreement and meetings

**UNFCCC (UN Framework Convention on Climate Change):** The international treaty adopted in 1992 that forms the legal roof over climate negotiations. The word "framework" matters: the convention sets the broad objective and institutions, and leaves detailed rules to later agreements and decisions.

**COP (Conference of the Parties):** The supreme decision-making body, where countries that have joined the convention meet every year. The Antalya meeting is the 31st, hence COP31.

**CMA:** The meeting of the parties to the Paris Agreement. It runs in the same place and the same fortnight as the COP, and today it carries most of the substantive work. Antalya hosts CMA8.

**Paris Agreement:** Adopted in 2015, it aims to hold warming "well below 2°C" above pre-industrial levels and to pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C. Its key innovation is that each country sets its own targets and is expected to raise them over time.

**Consensus:** COP decisions are usually not voted on. They are adopted when no party openly objects, which is one reason final-night bargaining can drag on.

## Targets and plans

**1.5°C:** The more ambitious temperature limit in the Paris Agreement. It refers to long-term average warming, not the temperature of any single year. Scientific assessments show that the difference between 1.5°C and 2°C matters for heatwaves, sea level and ecosystems. United Nations University's pre-COP31 briefing notes that temporarily exceeding 1.5°C ("overshoot") is now central to the discussion.

**NDC (Nationally Determined Contribution):** Each country's self-defined plan for cutting emissions and adapting. Plans are renewed every five years, and each new one is expected to be more ambitious than the last. The latest round covers targets to 2035 and was due in 2025.

**Net zero:** The point at which greenhouse gases released are balanced by those removed, through forests or technology. Türkiye's target year is 2053.

**Mitigation and adaptation:** Mitigation means cutting emissions to limit warming. Adaptation means preparing for impacts that can no longer be avoided, such as drought or floods. Finance for the two is negotiated separately.

**Global Goal on Adaptation (GGA):** A goal for measuring collective progress on adaptation. COP30 adopted 59 indicators for it; the details are still being negotiated.

<div class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> If you see "NDC 3.0" in the news, it refers to the third round of national plans under the Paris Agreement, the ones containing 2035 targets.</div>

## Review and accountability

**Global Stocktake (GST):** A collective, five-yearly review of how close the world is to the Paris goals. The first concluded at COP28 in Dubai in 2023. According to the UNFCCC, the second starts at CMA8 in November 2026 and concludes in 2028. Its findings are meant to shape the next round of NDCs.

**Transparency framework:** The system under which countries report their emissions and progress using common rules. It sounds dry, but it is the basis of trust: you cannot compare what you cannot measure.

## Money: finance, loss and damage

**Climate finance:** Public and private money flowing to mitigation and adaptation in developing countries. It is the most sensitive topic in the talks, tied directly to debates about historical responsibility and fairness.

**NCQG (New Collective Quantified Goal):** The new finance goal adopted at COP29 in Baku in 2024. It sets a target of at least USD 300 billion a year by 2035 for developing countries, with developed countries taking the lead. It also calls on all actors to scale up finance from all public and private sources to at least USD 1.3 trillion a year by 2035. Mixing up these two numbers is a common mistake.

**Loss and damage:** Dealing with climate impacts that adaptation cannot prevent, such as destruction from extreme weather or permanent losses. The dedicated Fund for responding to Loss and Damage was agreed at COP27 in Egypt in 2022 and made operational in the following years. It began accepting funding requests in December 2025 under an initial allocation of USD 250 million.

## Carbon markets: Article 6

Article 6 of the Paris Agreement sets the rules for countries to transfer emission reductions between them.

- **Article 6.2:** Two or more countries agree bilaterally to trade mitigation outcomes. The key concept is the **corresponding adjustment**: the selling country subtracts the reduction from its own accounts so the same tonne is not counted twice.
- **Article 6.4:** A centralised crediting mechanism under UN supervision, also called the Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism (PACM). Its core standards were adopted at COP29.

## Quick reference table

| Term | In short | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| UNFCCC | 1992 framework treaty | Legal roof over all talks |
| Paris Agreement | 2015; well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C | Foundation of today's system |
| NDC | National plan renewed every five years | Where targets actually live |
| GST | Five-yearly global review | Second one runs 2026–2028 |
| Loss and damage fund | Support for unavoidable impacts | Critical for the most vulnerable |
| Article 6 | Transfers of reductions between countries | Rules to avoid double counting |
| NCQG | At least USD 300bn/yr by 2035 | Reference point for finance debates |

<div class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> When a story quotes a figure, ask three questions: Who provides it? By what year? Public or private? Finance numbers can be read very differently until those are clear.</div>

<div class="faq"><details><summary>What is the difference between the COP and the CMA?</summary><p>The COP is the meeting of parties to the 1992 Framework Convention; the CMA is the meeting of parties to the Paris Agreement. They meet at the same venue in the same fortnight but take decisions under different treaties.</p></details><details><summary>Are NDCs binding?</summary><p>Preparing, submitting and updating an NDC is an obligation under the Paris Agreement. But countries decide the content of their targets, and there is no international penalty for missing them.</p></details><details><summary>Are Article 6 credits the same as the voluntary carbon market?</summary><p>No. Article 6 sets rules for transfers between countries and for the UN-supervised mechanism. The voluntary carbon market is a separate space where companies buy credits for their own pledges; the two interact, but the rules differ.</p></details></div>
