Almost everyone planning a technology startup in Türkiye eventually faces the same question: "Should we move into a technopark?" Technoparks, known locally as *teknokent*, offer a working environment closely tied to universities, along with substantial tax and employment incentives for R&D and software work. Those incentives come with conditions, limits and expiry dates, and the rules change over time. I am myself a co-founder of a technology company based at METU Technopark (ODTÜ Teknokent) in Ankara. In this post I outline what a technopark is, the typical steps to get in, and the main incentive categories, based on legislation in force as of 6 November 2026.

<div class="callout"><strong>Note:</strong> This post is general information only and is not legal or tax advice. For your own situation, talk to your technopark's management company and a certified accountant.</div>

## What is a technopark?

Officially called a **Technology Development Zone** (TGB), it is governed by **Law No. 4691 on Technology Development Zones**, adopted on 26 June 2001. Under the law's definition, these are areas where firms using high or advanced technology develop technology or software by drawing on the resources of a university, high-technology institute or R&D centre, and where academic, economic and social life come together. According to the Ministry of Industry and Technology, Türkiye now has more than 100 technoparks.

Each zone has a **management company**. By law, its founders must include at least one university, high-technology institute or public R&D centre. The management company evaluates the R&D or design projects of firms that want to move in, allocates space to those it approves, and checks that staff claiming incentives actually work in the zone.

## Typical steps to get in

Details vary between technoparks, but the process usually runs like this:

1.  **Define your project:** incentives rest on an "R&D, software or design project". That means not ordinary commercial activity but a project with technical uncertainty and an innovation goal.
2.  **Choose a technopark:** consider location, the strengths of the affiliated university, incubation programmes and office costs. Some zones are specialised in particular sectors.
3.  **Apply:** fill in the management company's project application form, covering the project's aim, what is new about it, the method, the team and the timeline.
4.  **Evaluation:** reviewers or a committee appointed by the management company assess the project; some ask for a presentation.
5.  **Space and company:** after approval you sign a lease, and either set up a new company or open a branch of an existing one in the zone.
6.  **Operations and reporting:** regular duties begin, such as staff notifications, monthly activity reports and keeping R&D costs separate from other activities.

For founders at the idea stage without a company, many technoparks run an **incubation centre**. The law provides that the zone's supports and exemptions also apply in these incubation centres.

## Main incentive categories

The table below summarises the main incentives under Law No. 4691 and related legislation. Amounts and rates change often, so I stay at a general level.

| Incentive | Scope (general) | Valid until |
|---|---|---|
| Income exemption | Earnings from software, design and R&D activities in the zone are exempt from income and corporate tax | 31 December 2028 |
| Payroll income tax incentive | Income tax on the wages of R&D, design and support staff for those duties is cancelled, within statutory limits | 31 December 2028 |
| Stamp duty | Documents for these wages are exempt from stamp duty, within statutory limits | 31 December 2028 |
| Social security support | Half of the employer's social security contribution for eligible staff is covered (via Law No. 5746) | 31 December 2028 |
| VAT exemption | Certain deliveries of software and services developed in the zone (VAT Law, provisional Article 20) | 31 December 2028 |
| Customs and fees | Goods imported for R&D projects are exempt from customs duty, funds, stamp duty and fees | No end date in the law |

The duration of the income and payroll incentives was extended from 31 December 2023 to 31 December 2028 by Law No. 7263 of 28 January 2021. Whether they will be extended again beyond 2028 depends on future legislation.

Some limits and recent changes worth knowing:

-   **Support staff cap:** the number of support staff benefiting from payroll and social security incentives cannot exceed 10% of R&D and design staff; for firms with up to 15 employees, the limit is 20%.
-   **2025 change:** Law No. 7555 of 20 July 2025 introduced a ceiling on the payroll incentive; it is now calculated on the portion of wages up to 40 times the gross minimum wage.
-   **Venture capital obligation:** firms whose exempt earnings exceed a set threshold must invest a portion in venture capital funds or startups; otherwise part of the exemption is lost.
-   **Working outside the zone:** time spent outside the zone counts towards incentives only within set ratios and with management company approval; those ratios can be raised for certain professions by presidential decision.

<div class="callout"><strong>Tip:</strong> The precondition for using incentives is good record-keeping. Tracking R&D project income and costs, who worked how much on which project, and every notification to the management company from day one is your best protection in a later tax audit.</div>

## Common mistakes

-   **Assuming all income is exempt:** the exemption covers only earnings from software, design and R&D activities in the zone. Activities outside the zone or income such as hardware sales fall under the general rules.
-   **Confusing the project with the business:** work unrelated to the approved project may not qualify, even if done in the zone.
-   **Misclassifying staff:** the line between R&D and support staff, and the ratio caps, directly affect the incentive calculation.
-   **Neglecting notifications:** for some incentives, such as the VAT exemption, notifications and approvals from the management company matter.

## Is a technopark the right choice for everyone?

The incentives are attractive, but a technopark is not the right home for every startup. If your product is mostly R&D and software, your team will grow, and you want to work with a university, it is a strong option. If your activity is mainly sales, consulting or services without real R&D content, the incentives may add little and the reporting burden may feel heavy. Before deciding, talk to the management companies of a few technoparks and ask an accountant to model the incentives against your own numbers.
